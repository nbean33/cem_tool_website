Short answer

CST Studio Suite is a broad 3D EM package leased at roughly 14,500 to 22,000 USD per year by published figures. For planar PCB RF, RayRF is a GPU FDTD tool at 1,499 USD per year with a GUI and live results. It does much less than CST overall, and it does the PCB slice fast.

CST's scope is 3D geometry, multiple solvers, and multiphysics, with a price to match. The question for a PCB RF designer is whether the work needs that scope, or whether it is patches, filters, couplers, and transmission lines that a focused tool turns around in minutes.

What CST has that RayRF does not

  • Arbitrary 3D geometry, not just planar PCB stackups.
  • Multiple solver types and multiphysics coupling.
  • A deep feature set with decades of industrial use.

If the work needs those, RayRF is not a replacement. CST's time-domain solver also uses the finite integration technique, closely related to FDTD, so the difference is scope and workflow rather than physics family.

What RayRF does

One job: planar PCB RF, fast. Draw or import the board, set the stackup and ports, slide a quality preset, and run on your NVIDIA GPU with S-parameters updating live. The whole loop is one window, and an edit-and-rerun cycle is seconds, not a context switch into a separate post-processor.
